TracAnything 2.0 x Georgia Tech: FP&A Redefined

May 4, 2025

Spring Semester (January - May 2025) | Atlanta, Georgia

At SalesHub, innovation drives us to push boundaries. This spring, we partnered with 30 exceptional MSA students from Georgia Tech’s Master of Science in Analytics program to elevate TracAnything 2.0, our all-in-one FP&A and PMO solution. Together, we’ve reimagined project accounting and financial planning for a modern, global business landscape.

Over the past semester, 30+ Georgia Tech MSA students partnered with SalesHub to expand TracAnything—our Salesforce-native FP&A platform. This hands-on initiative gave students direct access to real product development cycles while pushing our platform's capabilities forward.

🔍 The Scope: Five Teams, Five Core Missions

Each team owned a mission-critical area of FP&A, building tools that enhance how companies manage their finances at every level.

  • Financial Projections Students built forecasting models that project budgets month-by-month using Python and advanced analytics that reached up to 95% accuracy in predicting. These tools help businesses plan by department, geography, and timeline.
  • FP&A Integration This team focused on building variance analysis dashboards, scenario planning tools, and KPI trackers that supported real-time variance analysis and allowed for scenario modeling with 3+ dynamic inputs.
  • Automated Cost Categorization By training machine learning models on labeled data, students automated the classification of CapEx vs. OpEx, saving time and improving accuracy.
  • Data Preparation & Cleaning Teams wrangled and structured messy raw data, creating clean datasets that fed directly into analytics models and dashboards.
  • Project Coordination & Delivery This team acted as the glue—managing sprints, tracking progress, facilitating presentations, and ensuring final deliverables aligned with business needs.

🔧 The Process: From Paper to Platform

We didn’t just hand over requirements—we guided students through a full product lifecycle:

  1. Drafted ideas and whiteboarded workflows They began by visualizing how their solution would function in real-world finance teams.
  2. Built data models from scratch Using Python, Salesforce, and external tools, they structured logic around forecasting, categorization, and planning.
  3. Generated mock datasets These simulated real finance workflows, helping test edge cases before hitting production.
  4. Developed directly inside Salesforce Students pushed solutions into Salesforce orgs using Apex, Flows, and Lightning components.
  5. Delivered midterm demos and final presentations Each team shared their progress and live prototypes with stakeholders and mentors for feedback and iteration.

What’s New in TracAnything 2.0?

  • Smart Categorization: Automatically classify CapEx and OpEx transactions with over 90% accuracy. streamlining project accounting workflows.
  • AI-Driven Forecasting: Use machine learning and Python to build highly accurate budget predictions that proved 95% accurate during testing, enabling planning by month, department, and location.
  • Historical Insights: Analyze multi-year trends to make data-driven decisions for future projects.
  • Streamlined Month-End Processes: Simplify expense tracking, invoice management, and reviews across entire project portfolios. Cut down manual processing time by up to 40% through automation of expense rollups and invoice tracking.

This collaboration showcases what’s possible when local talent and global ambition converge. Using Salesforce as a foundation, we are committed to delivering scalable solutions that empower teams worldwide while leveraging insights from our community.

📣 Why This Matters

TracAnything 2.0 isn’t just an upgrade—it’s a real-world case study in applied analytics. In just 12 weeks, 30+ students:

  • Built production-ready solutions inside Salesforce
  • Delivered tools that reduced financial ops workload
  • Showed how AI, automation, and local talent can reshape enterprise systems

The Approach: Two Phases of Development

We tackled this project in two phases to ensure we didn’t rush and that everything was tested properly before moving to the next step.

  • Phase 1: We started by building our financial forecasting models using Excel. This gave us a clear idea of the financial predictions we wanted to implement and how they would behave under different conditions.
  • Phase 2: The next step was integrating those models into the TracAnything platform within Salesforce. We ensured everything worked together smoothly while leveraging Salesforce’s powerful analytics and forecasting capabilities.

Phase 1: Building the Forecasting Models

Starting with Excel allowed us to focus on the mechanics of the models before building them into Salesforce. Here’s how we approached it:

  1. Rolling Forecasts: We wanted to keep track of real-time project expenses and compare them to projections. This rolling forecast would allow project managers to see financial progress and make adjustments as needed.
  2. Future Budget Predictions: For larger-scale projects, I wanted to allow users to predict future expenses and revenues for longer periods (1-2 years). This was essential for teams looking to make informed decisions about their project pipelines.
  3. Revenue Forecasting: In addition to managing expenses, it was crucial that users could predict revenue for each project. This would provide a clearer picture of project financials, helping businesses balance both sides of their budget.

We set up a mock dataset in Mockaroo, where we generated 100 rows of random data. This dataset let us test our models before moving them into the Salesforce environment.

Phase 2: Integrating the Models into TracAnything

Once the Excel models were solid, we moved to Salesforce to incorporate them directly into TracAnything. This is where things got more technical. I had the team use various forecasting techniques, such as:

  • Linear Trend Forecasting: To identify long-term patterns in project costs.
  • Moving Average Forecasting: A technique used to smooth out any short-term fluctuations, making it easier to spot trends.
  • Exponential Smoothing: To give more weight to recent data, which is crucial for projects with unpredictable financial behavior.
  • Time Series Forecasting: I also had the team use Excel’s built-in time series forecasting capabilities to help with predicting seasonal trends, which worked perfectly for projects that have recurring financial patterns.

By using these techniques, we were able to create a system that not only tracks project expenses but also predicts future costs and revenues. This will enable users to make proactive decisions instead of reactive ones.
